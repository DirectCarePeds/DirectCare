Texting Your Pediatrician for Fevers — What Parents Should Know

Fevers are common, and they cause lots of parents to worry. What do we do about a fever? Is 101°F okay? Should we go to urgent care, or can this wait until morning?

The truth is, most fevers in kids are manageable at home — and texting your pediatrician about a fever is often all it takes to get peace of mind, without a clinic visit.

Here's what you need to know about managing fevers at home, and how texting a doctor can fit into the picture.

Photo by Annie Spratt on Unsplash

What Is a Fever?

A fever is your child's body doing exactly what it's supposed to do — fighting off an infection. In general, a fever is a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.

Most fevers are caused by common viruses and aren't dangerous on their own. What matters more than the number on the thermometer is how your child is acting.

How to Manage a Fever at Home

For most kids, home care is enough:

  • Offer plenty of fluids (water, electrolyte drinks, or breastmilk or formula)
  • Dress your child lightly — don't bundle up
  • Use fever-reducing medicine like acetaminophen or ibuprofen if your child is uncomfortable (always follow weight-based dosing, and ibuprofen is only for babies 6 months and older)
  • Let your child rest as needed
  • Keep an eye on how they're acting between fever spikes

Fever itself isn't usually the danger — dehydration and discomfort are what you're watching for.

When to Text Your Pediatrician

You don't have to wait until a fever feels severe to reach out.

Texting your doctor about a fever early — even if it's mild or new — helps us:

  • Understand what's going around and what to watch for
  • Catch small changes before they become bigger problems
  • Give you specific guidance for your child's age and history
  • Decide together whether home care, a virtual visit, or an in-person visit makes sense

Good things to include in your text:

  • Your child's age
  • The exact temperature and how it was taken
  • How long the fever has lasted
  • What you've already tried (fever reducers, fluids, etc.)
  • Other symptoms (cough, rash, vomiting, ear pain, etc.)
  • How your child is acting — playful, tired, fussy, or "just not right"

A photo or short video can help too. See our guide on what photos to send your pediatrician.

Signs That Warrant a Closer Look

A fever in an infant under 3 months old needs to be evaluated right away. Don't wait for a text reply — call us, or go to urgent or emergency care.

For older children, reach out to a doctor if you notice:

  • A fever lasting more than 4–5 days
  • Fever that improves and then returns
  • Signs of dehydration (no wet diapers/urination for 8+ hours, no tears when crying, very dry mouth)
  • Difficulty breathing or breathing that looks labored
  • Unusual sleepiness, confusion, or a child who "doesn't seem like themselves"
  • A febrile seizure

Any of these are a good reason to text us right away, or seek in-person care if something feels urgent.

The Bottom Line

Fevers are stressful, but they don't have to mean a rushed trip to urgent care every time. A quick text with your pediatrician can help you understand what's going on, what to watch for, and whether your child needs to be seen.

And remember: you never need to wait until things feel "bad enough" to text us. Reaching out early is exactly what we want you to do.
