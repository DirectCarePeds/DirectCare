Holistic Pediatrician in Utah: Evidence-Based Care With a Whole-Child Approach

Parents sometimes ask us, "Are you a holistic pediatrician?"

The answer is yes—but probably not in the way you might think.

At Direct Care Pediatrics in Sandy, Utah, we practice evidence-based pediatric medicine. We prescribe medications when children need them. We order labs and imaging. We treat infections. We refer to specialists. We can suture a cut, evaluate a sick newborn, manage ADHD, or help a family figure out whether their child needs the ER.

But we also believe medicine doesn't always have to be the first answer.

Sometimes a child needs an antibiotic. Sometimes they need time, fluids, sleep, good food, sunshine, movement, and a parent who knows what to watch for.

To us, that is what holistic pediatrics should mean.

What Does Holistic Pediatric Care Mean?

Holistic pediatric care means looking at the entire child instead of treating one symptom at a time.

When we're trying to understand why a child doesn't feel well, we may talk about nutrition, sleep, activity, development, school, stress, family history, medications, supplements, environment, and what has changed recently.

And then we ask an important question:

Does this child actually need medical treatment, or can we safely support their body while it gets better?

Children's bodies are remarkably good at healing. A big part of good pediatrics is knowing when to intervene—and when not to.

We Don't Believe Every Childhood Illness Needs a Prescription

  • A child with a fever doesn't automatically need fever-reducing medicine.
  • A child with a cold doesn't need an antibiotic.
  • Many mild ear infections can improve without antibiotics.
  • A positive test doesn't always tell the whole story without considering the child's symptoms and examination.

Sometimes the best medical care is careful observation, supportive care, and knowing exactly what signs would mean it's time to intervene.

That doesn't mean we're anti-medication or anti-antibiotic. Far from it. When a child needs treatment, we treat them.

We simply believe medications should have a good reason.

Food, Sleep, Movement and Lifestyle Are Part of Pediatrics

We would rather start with the basics whenever it makes sense.

Is the child sleeping enough? Are they breathing out of their mouth or nose? Are they eating foods that give them the nutrients they need? Are they getting outside? Are they moving their body? Could constipation be contributing to their stomachaches? Could sleep be affecting behavior and attention? Is there an underlying nutritional deficiency worth investigating?

Those aren't "alternative medicine" questions. They're pediatric medicine.

Sometimes lifestyle changes are enough. Sometimes they are one piece of a larger medical treatment plan. The point is that they shouldn't be ignored simply because writing a prescription is faster.

Can I Talk to My Pediatrician About Supplements and Natural Remedies?

Absolutely.

Parents ask us about probiotics, vitamins, supplements, herbal products, home remedies, and things they've seen online all the time.

You don't have to hide those questions from us.

We'll talk about what we know, what evidence exists, what we don't know, possible risks or interactions, and whether there might be a better option.

  • Sometimes we'll say, "Sure, that's reasonable."
  • Sometimes we'll say, "There isn't much evidence that this works, but here's what we know."
  • And sometimes we'll tell you that something isn't safe for a child.

Our goal isn't to make parents feel silly for asking. It's to give families good information so they can make informed decisions.

Holistic Doesn't Mean Anti-Medicine

This distinction is important.

Holistic pediatrics and modern medicine don't have to be opposites.

We believe in evidence. We believe antibiotics can be lifesaving or limb saving at times. We believe in emergency departments, specialists, imaging, laboratory testing, surgery, and modern medical treatment when children need them.

We also believe in avoiding unnecessary medications, unnecessary antibiotics, unnecessary visits, and unnecessary trips to urgent care when a child can safely be cared for at home.

Good medicine isn't about choosing "natural" medicine or "traditional" medicine.

It's about choosing the right amount of medicine for your child.

A Pediatrician You Can Actually Ask

One reason this approach works particularly well with direct pediatric care is access.

A lot of pediatric questions don't need a full doctor's appointment.

  • "My baby's poop suddenly looks like this—is it normal?"
  • "Do I need to give Tylenol for this fever?"
  • "Does this rash look concerning?"
  • "She's had a cough for four days. When should I worry?"
  • "Should we try this supplement?"

Parents can text Direct Care Pediatrics and start with the question.

When something needs an examination, we can arrange an office visit. Depending on the situation and service, we also provide virtual pediatric visits and house calls. If a child needs labs, imaging, medication, or specialty care, we can help families figure out the next step.

That continuity lets us do something that's surprisingly difficult in traditional healthcare: use less healthcare when less healthcare is what the child needs—and quickly escalate when it isn't.
